If you got most of these

Then you've got the thing this site was built to hand over, which was never a list of architectures. It's the habit of asking what kind of feedback a problem gives you, how much of it there is, and what failure will look like before you have a name for it. The specific layer types, the current best optimiser, whichever architecture is fashionable this year: that's all detail you can look up in ten minutes.

Where to go from here

The code here is small and meant to be read, and the most useful next step is to change it rather than read more of it. A few that are genuinely within reach:

  • Give the ViT a second attention block instead of a second head (the heads are already there, and one of them beats four at this scale). Two stacked blocks is a different question entirely: does depth help where width didn't?
  • Give the Rocket League cars a boost action: a fourth output and a fuel counter in the observation. Then watch how much the reward shaping has to change to accommodate it.
  • Replace the racer's mutation with crossover between two parents, and measure whether it actually helps on the Grand tour. Plenty of textbook genetic algorithms assume it does.
  • Swap the CNN's max pooling for average pooling and explain the accuracy difference from what you know about what pooling throws away.
  • Add a fifth dataset to the foundations playground that no architecture on that page can solve, and work out precisely why not.
